Bee-A-Hero: counting pollinator visits from orchard video

Team project, Ironhack Data + AI bootcamp, 2026

Problem
Growers can’t easily measure how often pollinators actually visit their flowers. Counting by eye is slow, and simple per-frame detection counts the same bee many times.
Approach
I built a training set of 151,545 labelled images across 2,526 insect classes, split 70/15/15 with no leakage between splits. On top of it I trained YOLO26-m detectors for flowers and five insect types and tracked each insect with BoT-SORT. A visit only counts when an insect stays on a flower for at least 2 seconds.
My part
Main contributor (142 of 179 commits). I built the data pipeline, the detection and tracking engine, and the FastAPI and React web app that turns an uploaded video into annotated footage and visit statistics.
Validation results
Flower detector, mAP@0.50.808
Insect detector (5 classes), mAP@0.50.669
20 test videos: landing episodes, then real visits155 → 31

Limits: the honeybee sub-classifier reaches F1 0.52 because it had only 168 training images. The yield model is designed but not built yet.

Python, PyTorch, Ultralytics YOLO, BoT-SORT, OpenCV, FastAPI, React, Docker

Wildfire risk forecasting for 16 Azerbaijani cities

Team of five, 10-day sprint, 2026

Problem
Estimate each city’s daily chance of a satellite-detected fire up to 30 days ahead, using only weather data and past fire records.
Approach
We pulled ERA5 weather from Open-Meteo and fire detections from NASA FIRMS, then engineered more than 200 features, including fire-weather indices, lags and rolling statistics. We compared 14 models and tuned CatBoost with Optuna, then calibrated its probabilities and explained it with SHAP. Train, validation and test sets were split by time, with 2025 held out as the test year.
My part
The data-ingestion pipeline, the multi-model fire classifier and its calibration, and the design of the shared src/ module that all six notebooks import.
Test set (2025)
ROC-AUC0.89
Recall on fire days0.80
Precision0.26
PR-AUC0.42

Why precision is low: only about 1 day in 12 is a fire day. We set the decision threshold so the model catches at least 70% of them, because a missed fire costs more than a false alarm.

Python, pandas, CatBoost, XGBoost, LightGBM, Optuna, SHAP, Prophet, Folium, GitHub Actions

Fastener defect inspection

Solo project, 2026

Problem
Sort photos of screws, nuts and washers into good and defective, across six classes, for a quality-control line.
Approach
I started with a classical OpenCV baseline (CLAHE, Otsu thresholding, contour and Hu-moment features). I then fine-tuned an ImageNet-pretrained ConvNeXt-Tiny with AdamW, a cosine schedule, mixed precision, RandAugment and flip test-time augmentation, and used Grad-CAM to check what the model attends to.
Held-out test set, 450 images
ConvNeXt-Tiny, macro-F10.989
Classical OpenCV baseline, part-type accuracy0.41

Limits: the dataset is small and evenly balanced, with clean backgrounds. Real factory images would need a fresh evaluation.

Python, PyTorch, timm, OpenCV, scikit-learn

Solar filament segmentation, Kaggle and IEEE BigData Cup 2026

Solo, in progress

Instance segmentation of solar filaments on 2048×2048 H-alpha images from the GONG telescope network. A Mask R-CNN finds each filament on the full disk, then a U-Net redraws it from a full-resolution crop. Validation folds are grouped by observation day, because the same filament appears for several days and from several sites.

On a small local check (80 images), panoptic quality rose from 0.34 to 0.40 between versions 2 and 3. There is no leaderboard result yet, and the code stays private until the competition ends.
